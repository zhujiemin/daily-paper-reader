## ABSTRACT 

Generated audio is now used in a range of applications, creating a need to verify its origin after distribution and signal processing. This task is particularly chal-lenging for autoregressive audio generation because codec processing can alter the token sequence recovered from the waveform. Such changes reduce the relia-bility of watermark detection and payload decoding. Existing methods construct token groups using either intrinsic token representations or substitution patterns caused by transformations. As a result, intrinsic token relationships and codec induced substitutions are modeled separately. In addition, most methods support only zero bit detection. They can determine whether a watermark is present but cannot distinguish individual generated outputs. We propose MARC , a multi-bit generative watermarking method for autoregressive audio generation. MARC in-tegrates intrinsic token representations with confusion patterns obtained through retokenization and multiple codecs, forming a codec-aware token-cluster space. Within this space, payload-driven cluster scheduling is used to embed a multi-bit watermark, while detection and payload decoding are performed on retokenized observations. Experiments on speech, dialogue, and music generation show that MARC achieves an average of 97.3% bit extraction accuracy on unmodified water-marked audio and the watermark can still be extracted under diverse codec attacks. MARC also demonstrates robustness to overwriting attacks. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Autoregressive models are widely used for audio generation (Rouard et al., 2026), including speech synthesis (Nishimura et al., 2025), spoken dialogue (Tan et al., 2026), and music generation (Liu et al., 2025), where discrete audio representations allow different generation tasks to be formulated as token-sequence modeling. As these models are increasingly used in practical applications, gen-erated audio needs not only reliable source verification but also the ability to distinguish and trace individual outputs (San Roman et al., 2024). Multi-bit generative watermarking provides this ca-pability by embedding additional information into each generated audio sample beyond zero-bit detection. This information should remain recoverable after the audio is released. In reality, gen-erated audio may undergo compression or other codec processing during transmission, storage, and redistribution (Liu et al., 2024a). Verification for autoregressive token-level watermarking requires retokenizing the generated waveform, and the resulting token sequence may differ from the one used during generation. Codec processing can further increase these token substitutions, making both watermark detection and multi-bit recovery more difficult. Existing work has developed several forms of generative watermarking for autoregressive and token-based audio generation as shown in Figure 1. Wu et al. (2025a) introduce statistical token-level watermarking and identifies retokenization mismatch. Aligned-IS (Wu et al., 2025b) and Hidden in Plain Tokens (HiPT) (Milis et al., 2026) further improve robustness through token grouping, but two limitations remain. First, single-source token grouping : existing clustering strategies rely on a sin-gle source of token relationships: Aligned-IS groups tokens using intrinsic representations, whereas HiPT derives groups from transformation-induced confusion, so these two types of information are not modeled jointly. Second, zero-bit limitation : existing autoregressive watermarking primar-ily targets zero-bit detection, which can detect watermark but cannot carry additional information 1arXiv:2610.11488v1 [cs.CR] 8 Oct 2026 for distinguishing individual generated outputs. Extending such methods to multi-bit watermark-ing is difficult because retokenization and codec processing can directly corrupt the embedded bits. To solve these problems, we propose MARC , a generative watermarking framework that enables robust multi-bit embedding for autoregressive audio generation. To address single-source token grouping , MARC combines intrinsic token representations with confusion patterns induced by re-tokenization and diverse codecs to build a codec-aware token-cluster space. To address zero-bit limitation , MARC uses payload-driven cluster scheduling to embed a multi-bit watermark and per-forms detection and decoding on retokenized observations to recover both watermark presence and the embedded bits. MARC additionally introduces a public check value (pub-key) and a secret auxiliary suffix (pri-key) for message consistency checking, with both parameters used in the full verification and recovery protocol without assuming cryptographic security. Across multiple autore-gressive audio generation tasks, MARC achieves an average of 97.3% bit extraction accuracy on unmodified watermarked audio and the watermark can still be extracted under diverse codec attacks. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We propose MARC , a codec-robust multi-bit generative watermarking framework for au-toregressive audio generation that extends zero-bit detection to multi-bit payload embed-ding and recovery. • We propose a codec-aware token-cluster representation that combines intrinsic token structure with codec-aware confusion, reducing the limitations of single-source token grouping under different codec transformations. • We develop payload-driven embedding and robust recovery for multi-bit watermarking under token-level errors. Experiments on speech, dialogue, and music generation show that MARC works across different autoregressive audio generation tasks and remains robust under codec attacks. • We demonstrate robustness to watermark overwriting , showing that MARC preserves detection and recovery of the original payload when an additional watermark is embedded into the released audio. ## 2 RELATED WORK 

Generative Audio Watermarking. Generative audio watermarking embeds watermark informa-tion during audio synthesis rather than adding it through post-hoc waveform modification. Existing methods integrate watermarks into diffusion generation (Cao et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2024b), latent representations (San Roman et al., 2025), diffusion noise distributions (Tang, 2025), model param-eters (Feng et al., 2025), or diffusion-domain modulation (Lee et al., 2025). These studies show that watermarking can be incorporated into different audio generators, but their mechanisms mainly depend on continuous latent, diffusion, or model-specific representations and are not directly appli-cable to discrete token sampling in autoregressive generation. Autoregressive audio watermarking instead operates on token distributions and must remain detectable after the generated waveform is retokenized. 2Autoregressive Audio Watermarking. TraceableSpeech (Zhou et al., 2024) generates water-marked speech within TTS, while Wu et al. (2025a) introduces statistical token-level watermarking for autoregressive speech and identifies retokenization mismatch as a central issue. Aligned-IS (Wu et al., 2025b) reduces this mismatch by clustering tokens according to intrinsic representation ge-ometry. However, its clustering does not explicitly consider codec attacks. HiPT (Milis et al., 2026) instead builds token groups from transformation-aware confusion, directly modeling token substitu-tions observed after processing, but does not jointly use intrinsic token structure. The two methods therefore rely on different sources of token relationships, but they do not model these sources jointly. In addition, existing autoregressive methods mainly focus on zero-bit detection rather than embed-ding multi-bit information. Joint modeling of token structure and codec-aware confusion with robust multi-bit recovery still remains insufficiently addressed. Our method combines these two sources of token information and extends autoregressive generative watermarking to multi-bit embedding and recovery. 

## 3 THREAT MODEL 

Adversary Goal. We consider a waveform yw generated by an autoregressive audio model and watermarked with a multi-bit payload m. The full verification protocol additionally uses a public check value (pub-key) and a secret auxiliary suffix (pri-key) for message consistency checking, and neither parameter is assumed to provide cryptographic meaning. After release, an output-level transformation A produces 

˜y = A(yw),

which is retokenized for watermark verification and payload recovery. The verifier uses m⋆ to search over candidate alignments and then estimates the payload from the selected observations. The adversary aims to cause missed detection or incorrect payload recovery while preserving the underlying audio content and perceptual usability. The same setting covers benign post-processing. 

Adversary Capability. The pri-key is not directly disclosed, only the owner and the authorized user can have it. The adversary does not control the verifier’s payload hypothesis m⋆. We con-sider neural audio codec attacks, signal processing, and desynchronization applied to the released waveform. These transformations can alter both token identities and their temporal correspondence with watermark embedding steps, even when perceptual content is preserved. MARC focuses on multi-bit verification and recovery under this joint token mismatch and temporal misalignment, with codec-induced retokenization mismatch as the primary challenge. Test-time codecs may include those unseen during construction of the codec-aware cluster map.

## 5 EVALUATION 

5.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

For our main audio generation experiments, we consider two audio generative models with autore-gressive architecture, that span different applications and use different audio codecs for discretiza-tion. For dialogue and speech synthesis tasks, We evaluate MARC on SpiritLM (Nguyen et al., 2025) using Finance QA (Tu et al., 2024), Dolly CW (Conover et al., 2023), and LibriSpeech (Panayotov et al., 2015). For music generation tasks we evaluate on MusicGen (Copet et al., 2023) using Lib-riSpeech, MusicCaps (Agostinelli, 2023), and MTG-Jamendo (Bogdanov et al., 2019). We compare our method with KGW using δ = 2 .0 following Wu et al. (2025a) which we name as AR speech WM, Aligned-IS (Wu et al., 2025b), and HiPT (Milis et al., 2026). As for our method MARC, embeds a 12 bit payload by default. The main configuration assigns 8 consecutive water-mark steps to each symbol, cycles through the three positions, and repeats the schedule across 16 laps. We evaluate multi-bit watermark detection using bit accuracy as the detection score, True Positive Rate (TPR) is measured at a target False Positive Rate (FPR) of 1% . Cluster Median-p (cluster p) summarizes the cluster-based detection statistic. Bit accuracy (bit acc) is the fraction of correctly recovered payload bits. For fidelity, we report predicted MOS with NISQA (Mittag et al., 2021) and DNSMOS (Cumlin et al., 2024) scores for speech outputs. We compute FAD using VGGish features (Kilgour et al., 2019). NISQA and DNSMOS are not used to assess generated music. These two scores correspond to the naturalness of speech and noise levels, which do not measure musical quality, and therefore MusicGen’s results do not use these two metrics. 5.2 RELIABILITY AND FIDELITY

We evaluate MARC against two types of attacks: audio transformation attacks, which perturb the watermarked audio, and overwriting attacks, which introduce a competing watermark to challenge the original attribution. Experiments are conducted on SpiritLM and MusicGen. We consider 21 attacks spanning 9 codec and neural regeneration attacks, 8 signal processing attacks, and 4 desyn-chronization attacks. These transformations challenge token consistency, acoustic evidence preser-vation, and temporal alignment, respectively. As shown in Table 3, MARC outperforms all baselines on this metric under all nine codec and neural regeneration attacks on both models. This performance is consistent with the complementary roles of payload-driven scheduling and alignment-aware decoding, repeated observations preserve recovery evidence, while alignment cor-rection and cross-lap aggregation accommodate temporal disruption. Additional codec parameter evaluations and experimental details are provided in Figure 4 and Appendices A.2. For the overwriting attack, an overwriting attacker embeds a competing message to replace the orig-inal attribution. Table 2 shows that the legitimate owner can still recover the original payload using their private key, and the attacker’s message is also recoverable with the attacker’s key. This preser-vation of the original payload demonstrates MARC’s resistance to overwriting under the evaluated conditions and supports key-conditioned payload recovery. 5.5 ABLATION STUDY

For MARC, this evaluation focuses on whether multi-bit message recovery and corresponding ver-ification can distinguish between different attribution claims, and to what extent the distributed ev-idence of the original message can be preserved under active interference. Combined with codec attacks, the two types of experiments respectively test information retention capability under rep-resentational transformations and attribution reliability under competing watermarks, reflecting the method’s design objectives for audio re-encoding and active watermark replacement.
